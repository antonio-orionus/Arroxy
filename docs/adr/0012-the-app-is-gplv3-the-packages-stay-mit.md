# The app is GPLv3, the packages stay MIT

Arroxy was released under MIT from its first version. From this change on, the
app is GPL-3.0-or-later; `yt-dlp-bridge` and `ytdlp-errors` stay MIT.

MIT let anyone take the app, close it, and ship it as their own product with no
duty to share their changes. GPLv3 keeps every redistributed version, modified
or not, open under the same terms. It does not stop anyone from selling a copy,
and it binds only recipients, not the copyright holder: a separately licensed
paid edition stays possible. Most comparable consumer Electron apps are GPL or
AGPL (FreeTube, LosslessCut, Open Video Downloader, Joplin, Signal), while MIT
and Apache dominate developer tools. AGPL was rejected because Arroxy has no
network-served component for it to cover. "Or later" follows the FSF default
and keeps the code combinable with a future GPL.

The packages stay MIT because they are libraries: a copyleft license would
mostly stop other projects from using them.

Every release before this change stays MIT, and anyone may still fork it on
those terms. The few outside contributions were made under MIT, which permits
including them in a GPLv3 work; their MIT notices stay valid. Rebranded clones
are not a licensing problem: `TRADEMARKS.md` covers the name and logo.

Accepting outside GPL contributions means the code in a future closed paid
edition can only be code Antonio owns, unless contributors sign an agreement
granting that right. Decide on such an agreement before building a paid
edition, not after.
